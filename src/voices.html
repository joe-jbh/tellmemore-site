Setting up

Choosing a voice

Speak works the moment the app is installed. But the voice the iPad ships with is the small, robotic one, and a Bible read in that voice is hard to listen to for long. Ten minutes of setup gives it a voice you'd happily hear for an hour.

1. Download a better voice

This is the one step that matters. Here it is done on an iPad, choosing a Siri voice:

{{short:setup-voice}}

Step by step, on the iPad:

  1. Open Settings › Accessibility › Read & Speak › Voices › English.

  2. Pick a voice and tap the download arrow beside it. Choose one marked Premium if it's offered, or Enhanced — those are the natural-sounding ones. Downloads are a few hundred megabytes, so do it on Wi-Fi.

  3. Wait for the download to finish (the arrow becomes a tick). Voices are per device: a voice on an old iPad isn't on a new one.

Then in the app: Settings (the ⚙ button) › Read Aloud › pick the voice you downloaded. Hear this voice plays a line so you can compare. Slower and Faster set the pace.

Which voice?

Taste, mostly — but a few are consistently good for long reading in American English. Try these first:

Ava (Premium)A warm, unhurried woman's voiceThe one most people settle on for reading. Clear on names.
Evan (Premium)A calm, mid-range man's voiceGood for long passages; a little formal.

Samantha (Enhanced)The familiar iPad voice, improvedFine if it's what you're used to.

Avoid anything marked Compact, and the novelty voices (Bells, Cellos, Bad News). If the list in the app says only basic voices are installed, step 1 hasn't finished yet.

If you want a Siri voice

Siri's voices don't appear in any app's own list; the only way to hear one is through the iPad's own Read & Speak settings. So:

  1. In Settings › Accessibility › Read & Speak › Voices, choose a Siri voice (the Siri group at the top of the list) and download it.

  2. In the app's Read Aloud settings, leave the voice on the first choice — "use the device's Accessibility settings".

In that mode the speaking rate also comes from Read & Speak › Speaking Rate rather than the app's Slower / Faster, and Bible names are said the way Siri says them.

Names

Whichever voice you choose, the app tells it how to say the rare names — Habakkuk, Zerubbabel, Melchizedek — from an English pronouncing dictionary, so they come out the way they're read in church rather than as a guess. If a name sounds wrong to you, that's worth telling us: it becomes one line in a list.

Voices for the Spanish and Chinese Bibles

If you've downloaded the extra Bibles (Settings › Bibles in the app), Speak on a Spanish or Chinese card reads it in a voice for that language, not your English one. The iPad comes with only a basic voice for each, and it sounds robotic. To give them a natural voice:

  1. Open Settings › Accessibility › Read & Speak › Voices and choose Spanish (or Chinese).

  2. Download a voice marked Premium or Enhanced from the main list, not the Siri group, which apps can't use. For Spanish there are voices for Mexico, the United States and Spain; Mexican or US Spanish is what most American readers will expect. For Chinese, choose a Mandarin voice for China mainland: both Chinese Bibles are in simplified characters. Cantonese voices aren't used.

  3. That's all. The app picks the best voice installed for each language, and uses a new one as soon as it has downloaded.

Like the English voices, each is a few hundred megabytes; download on Wi-Fi. Only download the languages you'll listen to.

Two settings you don't need — but might like

  • Speak Selection (Settings › Accessibility › Read & Speak) adds a Speak button whenever you select text, anywhere on the iPad, not only in this app.
  • Speak Screen reads any screen aloud when you swipe down from the top with two fingers.

Neither affects the app's own Speak button; they're for the rest of the iPad.

The iPad's silent switch doesn't silence the app — it plays as spoken audio, like an audiobook. Only the volume buttons matter.

2. Turn on Apple Intelligence, for Ask

Ask finds verses, names, words and references on any iPad. On one that supports Apple Intelligence it also writes a short answer to the question, every sentence citing the entry it came from. The writing happens on the iPad itself; nothing is sent away to do it.

  1. Open Settings › Siri. (Before iPadOS 27 the page is called Apple Intelligence & Siri, with an Apple Intelligence switch: turn it on.)

  2. Check that Siri's Language is the same as the iPad's (Settings › General › Language & Region). Apple Intelligence only runs when they match.

  3. The first time, the iPad downloads what it needs, which can take a while — leave it on Wi-Fi and charging.

  4. If Screen Time is on, look under Content & Privacy Restrictions › Siri and make sure Apple Intelligence isn't restricted there.

The app checks when it opens. If something still needs doing it says so, and once Apple Intelligence is ready it says nothing at all. If this iPad doesn't support Apple Intelligence, the app tells you once, and nothing else changes: Ask still finds everything, and for a question about a passage it shows a short summary of the chapter prepared ahead of time, labelled as such.
