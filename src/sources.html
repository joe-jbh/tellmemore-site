What's in the app

Bibles and references

Everything the app reads from is on the device. Most of it is public domain: dictionaries, commentaries and concordances old enough that no one owns them. A few newer datasets are shared freely under a Creative Commons Attribution licence, and one under a BSD licence. Each is credited inside the app under Settings › About › Sources & Licenses, which also notes what this edition changed. Any of them can be exported from there as a plain text file.

Ask also has a short summary of each chapter, prepared ahead of time from Matthew Henry's Concise Commentary for iPads that can't write their own answers. It is labelled as prepared, and it cites the entries it was written from.

Five more commentaries, to download

The larger commentaries are a single download of about {{commentaries_download_mb}} MB, from Settings, so a reader who never opens one doesn't have to carry them. The app is complete without them, and you can remove them again. (The privacy page explains what the download does and doesn't send.)
